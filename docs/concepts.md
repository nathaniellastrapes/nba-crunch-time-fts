# Concepts

## Construct Validity
Construct validity questions how well our measure captures what we are hoping to capture. In order to validate the construct of "crunch time" we need to evaluate whether the definition of "a game within 5 points with less than 5 minutes" captures true crunch time. A validation concern is that a player can be shooting free throws with 5 minutes left vs. a tie game with 10 seconds left. Both of those are completely different pressure situations.

## Simpson's Paradox
Simpson's Paradox is a phenomenon where aggregated results can differ from overall results. For example, if good shooters and poor shooters respond to pressure differently, pooled free throw percentage can hide the true signal.

## Hypothesis Testing
Hypothesis testing assumes a null hypothesis (H₀) of "no effect" and computes the p-value: the probability of observing data at least as extreme as what we got, IF the null were true. Small p-value → reject H₀, conclude an effect exists. Large p-value → fail to reject, can't claim an effect (but doesn't prove no effect — could be insufficient power). Critical: with large samples, statistically significant differences can be practically negligible. Always inspect effect size alongside p-value.

## Confidence Intervals
A confidence interval gives you a range of plausible values for the true parameter. To interpret: read the lower and upper bounds in plain English. Note (a) whether the interval crosses zero (relates to significance), (b) the magnitude of the bounds (small CI = small effects ruled out and ruled in), and (c) where the point estimate sits. Always more informative than a p-value alone.
